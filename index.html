CH01 — About

Where Biology
Meets the World

I am Anubhuti T. Shukla — PGT Biology, Head of Science Department, and CBSE Subject Matter Expert based in Delhi, India. I believe that every chapter in your NCERT textbook is secretly about climate change, war, geopolitics, or a pandemic.


ATS — Biology × World is my attempt to break the wall between the classroom and the crisis. Between the exam and the real emergency. Between what you memorise and what you must understand to survive the 21st century.


This space is for students who want to think deeper, teachers who want to teach wider, and anyone who believes science education should produce citizens — not just exam toppers.

CH04 — Articles

Biology Stories
From the Headlines

Ecosystems × Politics
Why Your Lungs Are Paying India's Pollution Tax
📚 Class XI Ch.17 — Breathing & Exchange of Gases | Class XII Ch.16 — Environmental Issues

Every winter, stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana turns the Gangetic plain into a gas chamber. When PM2.5 spikes above 500 µg/m³, your alveoli are doing Chapter 17 in real time — except the NCERT exam doesn't ask who is responsible for the damage.

💭 If farmers burning stubble are the proximate cause of Delhi's air crisis, what is the ultimate cause — and who should pay to fix it?
Water × War
The War That Killed a River: Biology of the Euphrates
📚 Class XII Ch.14 — Ecosystem | Ch.16 — Environmental Issues

The Euphrates — cradle of civilisation — is running dry. Wars in Syria and Iraq, Turkish dams upstream, and climate change have reduced its flow by 40%. The aquatic biomes your textbook describes are collapsing. This is Chapter 14 with blood on its hands.

💭 When a river crosses borders, who owns its water — and what happens to the biology of every nation downstream when one nation dams it?
Genetics × Ethics
The Baby Was Edited: CRISPR and the Line We Just Crossed
📚 Class XII Ch.6 — Molecular Basis of Inheritance | Ch.11 — Biotechnology: Principles

In 2018, a Chinese scientist edited the germline of two human embryos. The twin girls carry permanent genetic changes that will pass to their children. Your textbook calls this "gene therapy." The rest of the world calls it a line that should never have been crossed.

💭 If germline editing can eliminate hereditary disease, what moral authority do we have to ban it — and what happens if only the wealthy can afford it?
Pandemic × Immunity
What COVID Left in Our Cells: The Long Haul Biology
📚 Class XII Ch.8 — Human Health & Disease | Ch.9 — Strategies for Enhancement

Four years after COVID, millions of people still cannot walk up a flight of stairs. The virus left viral RNA in immune cells, reprogrammed T-cell populations, and created auto-antibodies attacking the body's own tissues. The chapter ends. The suffering does not.

💭 If a government declares a pandemic over but millions of its citizens are biologically still in it — whose definition of "over" should we believe?
CH05 — Lessons

Teaching Biology
as a World Event

Lesson plans that connect NCERT chapters to global crises — designed to produce systems thinkers, not exam machines.

Class XII · Chapter 15 — Biodiversity · 2 Periods

Species Extinction as Political Crisis

Students analyse the IUCN Red List alongside UN biodiversity treaty failures. Real-world hook: India's cheetah reintroduction programme. Activity: Debate — should extinction be treated as a crime against humanity?

Class XI · Chapter 22 — Chemical Coordination · 2 Periods

Hormones, Plastics & the Endocrine Disruption Crisis

Connects the endocrine system to the global microplastics crisis. Real-world hook: declining global sperm counts. Activity: Case study analysis of BPA, phthalates, and regulatory failure across three countries.

Class X · Chapter 5 — Life Processes · 1 Period

Photosynthesis and the Politics of Carbon

From the light reaction to carbon markets — who profits from trees absorbing CO₂? Real-world hook: Amazon deforestation credits sold to corporations. Activity: Role play as nations negotiating at COP.

Global · IB / IGCSE Compatible · 3 Periods

Antibiotic Resistance as a Geopolitical Problem

Why pharmaceutical companies stopped developing new antibiotics — and what that means for global health security. Students map the economic incentive failure and propose policy solutions from a biology foundation.

Global · All Curricula · 2 Periods

The Biology of Famine: Why People Starve When Food Exists

Connects cellular respiration and nutrition to the political economy of food systems. Students analyse the 2022 Horn of Africa famine against global food export data — and ask who decided where the grain went.

Global · Climate Focus · 2 Periods

The Coral Reef as a Climate Witness

Using Great Barrier Reef bleaching data, students reconstruct the biology of symbiosis breakdown and connect it to national emissions data. Which countries caused the bleaching? Which countries suffer most from it?
